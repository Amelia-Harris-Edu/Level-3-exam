Before you start

What is the job?

This is not a “tell me everything you know about the film” essay. You choose ONE statement, decide how far you agree, and build a critical argument using close analysis.

Film evidence can be quotations, but it can also be precise observation: camera work, sound, editing, performance, mise-en-scène, setting, structure, symbolism and exact moments.

Paper practice: use this tool to prepare. When you reach the writing stage, put the screen aside and write by hand.

Step 2

Choose the statement that FITS your text

Read all the options. Choose the statement that gives your text the best opportunity for a thoughtful argument and several precise moments. Do not twist a prepared essay to fit.
Step 3

Unpack it

First make a judgement. How far do you agree? A Level 3 response evaluates the statement; it does not simply prove it true.

Step 4

Build your thesis

Your thesis is your answer. Make a judgement and reuse some language from the statement so your argument is unmistakable.
Complexity is useful when it is real. Words such as although, however, ultimately, more significantly, on the surface, yet can help you qualify a judgement.
Step 5

Plan three moves

Do not plan “three techniques”. Plan three things you want to prove. Techniques and moments are evidence.

Step 6

What does going deeper look like?

Achievement

Build a relevant critical argument, evaluate the statement, make a range of relevant points, and support them with specific details.

Merit

Make it convincing: show depth of knowledge, weave evidence into reasoning, and explore how / why the director's choices support your judgement.

Excellence

Make it sophisticated and insightful: develop and evaluate the argument, choose evidence judiciously, consider audience positioning, and make meaningful links to human experience, society or wider context.

2025 markers rewarded: tight alignment to the statement, precise evidence, director purpose, audience positioning and meaningful wider-world thinking.

They warned against: plot summary, generic emotion comments, simplistic technique spotting, confusing scene with setting, too much material, and twisting a pre-prepared essay to fit.
Step 7

Now write it on paper

Target: a concise response of no more than about 800–900 words / 5–6 pages. Quality matters more than length.

Put a tiny plan at the top of the page: thesis → P1 → P2 → P3. Then write. Keep the statement's keywords alive throughout.

You can use TEXAS, PEEL+, PETAL, TAKO or another structure. The marker is not grading the acronym. They are grading your critical argument.
